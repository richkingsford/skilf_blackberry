FAQ

Straight answers for interns, mentors, board members, and companies trying to understand how Skilf helps people prove tech-career readiness and pursue a first real tech job offer.

What is a Skilf?

A Skilf is proof that someone can defend a real project or skill in front of people who understand the work. It is earned through evidence, check-ins, and Demo Day review.

Why do you call candidates interns?

We use intern for anyone pursuing a Skilf. Some already work with an organization, and some are working on their own startup or project until a stronger internship appears.

How do monthly check-ins work?

Interns progress through four monthly check-ins. Each check-in is a short expert review that tests evidence, surfaces weak spots, and helps the intern decide what to improve before Demo Day.

What happens at Demo Day?

The intern demonstrates the work, answers expert questions, and receives a pass/fail decision from board members. Passing means the evidence was strong enough to earn the Skilf.

Can small companies hire or mentor interns?

Yes. We especially encourage small companies, including one-person companies, to collaborate with interns. Sending a message from an intern card is the easiest way to begin.

How do board members get paid?

Board members may volunteer or be paid. Monthly check-ins use a flat fee per 15-minute visit. Demo Day fees are flat per appearance and negotiated when a board member is hired.

Do check-ins require video?

The live check-in can happen by phone or Zoom, and cameras can be on or off. The official review artifact is always one public YouTube video, 30-60 seconds long, posted within 24 hours before the review.

How do I start?

Use Begin a Skilf in the top navigation or the application form to choose a role: intern, mentor, board member, hiring partner, company project poster, scholarship requester, or feedback sender.
